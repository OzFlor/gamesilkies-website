Why documentation matters

GameSilkies is not limited to breeding outcomes. Its documentation follows the actual development of individual birds and the breeding population over time.

Observations may concern physical development, body weight, behaviour, feeding, reproduction, health, environmental conditions and interactions between birds.

The purpose is to preserve a chronological record that can be revisited as further observations and evidence become available.

From observation to publication

The project's documentation process distinguishes the original observation from subsequent organisation and publication.

04 — Published record

Selected documentation is released through the project's public archives and platforms.

This process does not make AI the source of the original observations. Authorship and responsibility for the underlying work remain with the human observer.

Important distinction

A normalised log is an organised representation of original observations. It is not necessarily a complete reproduction of the private raw log.

Public documentation is selective. Unpublished material and private working records remain under the control of the project author.

Chronology and continuity

GameSilkies logs follow chronological continuity. A new log begins when the preceding log ends. The recording period is not necessarily a rigid 24-hour interval.

This approach avoids artificial boundaries that could separate related observations or interrupt the sequence of events.

Dates and times are retained as recorded whenever possible. Corrections, clarifications and later interpretations should not silently rewrite the original chronology.

Individual identification

Individual birds are important units of observation. Their histories should remain distinguishable throughout the documentation.

Records may include:

  • Individual names and identification references.
  • Known or estimated dates of birth.
  • Parentage and lineage information.
  • Body weight and development.
  • Physical characteristics and changes.
  • Behaviour and interactions.
  • Individual health observations and history.
  • Breeding participation and offspring.

Unknown or uncertain parentage and identification must remain identified as such. Assumptions should not become established facts simply through repetition.

Observations, interpretations and hypotheses

Not everything recorded is a confirmed explanation of what happened.

  • Observation: What was directly seen, measured or recorded.
  • Interpretation: An explanation or understanding based on available observations.
  • Hypothesis: A possible explanation that may require further investigation.
  • Uncertainty: Information that remains incomplete, ambiguous or unverified.

Interpretations may change when new evidence becomes available. The existence of an earlier interpretation should not be confused with confirmation of that interpretation.

Measurements and supporting material

Measurements and supporting material provide additional context for individual observations.

Depending on the subject, documentation may include:

  • Body weight and measurement records.
  • Photographs and original image files.
  • Video observations.
  • Feeding and husbandry records.
  • Environmental and weather observations.
  • Time references and measurement conditions.
  • Descriptions of instruments and methods.

Measurements should be understood in their recorded context. Instrument limitations, inconsistent conditions and possible measurement errors must not be overlooked.

AI-assisted documentation

Artificial intelligence may assist in organising large volumes of observations, improving structure, identifying chronology and preparing readable documentation.

AI assistance does not replace direct observation or independent verification.

AI must not be represented as having personally observed, handled, measured, bred or cared for any bird.

Uncertainty should be preserved rather than replaced with invented explanations, diagnoses, measurements or historical details.

The human author remains responsible for reviewing and deciding what is published.

Original imagery and preservation

Photographic documentation is an important part of GameSilkies. Images can preserve details that may be difficult to describe completely in text.

Original photographs and their associated metadata may provide useful information about dates, equipment, image properties and the history of documentation.

The project may supply selected original imagery directly to agreed collaborators or suitable preservation initiatives.

Original image files remain attributed to their author. Publicly available video versions should not automatically be considered substitutes for the original photographic material.

Continuity rather than finality

GameSilkies is an evolving documentation project. Records accumulate as birds develop, generations progress and new observations become available.

Not every observation will produce a conclusion. Not every hypothesis will be confirmed. Not every question will have an immediate answer.

The intention is to maintain a useful record of what was observed, when it happened and how understanding developed over time.
