Individual identity matters

GameSilkies documents individual birds rather than treating all hybrids as interchangeable examples of a single type.

Each bird has its own development, physical characteristics, behaviour and history. Individual identification makes it possible to follow those differences over time.

The project considers the individual bird an important unit of observation, both for practical husbandry and for understanding the outcomes of experimental crossbreeding.

Identification and project status

GameSilkies is an ongoing experimental project. Individual birds are documented according to their actual identity and available history.

Inclusion in the documentation does not automatically establish confirmed parentage, genetic composition or a particular generation. Such information depends on the evidence available for each bird.

What an individual record may contain

Identification

Name, identification reference, sex when known and distinguishing physical characteristics.

Origins

Hatch or birth date, parentage and available breeding history.

Development

Growth, body weight, feathering and physical changes over time.

Behaviour

Temperament, social interactions, activity and individual behavioural observations.

Health observations

Recorded physical conditions, health history, care and relevant observations without inventing diagnoses.

Breeding history

Breeding participation, offspring and subsequent generations where documented.

Generations and parentage

Individual identification is closely connected to the project's breeding documentation.

Where parentage is known, records can help establish relationships between birds and follow their descendants through subsequent generations.

Where parentage is uncertain, incomplete or disputed, that uncertainty must remain visible. An assumed relationship must not become a confirmed lineage merely through repetition.

Further information about breeding and generations is presented on the Breeding page.

Living birds and historical records

The project includes both living birds and historical individual records.

A bird's death does not end the historical value of its documentation. Previous measurements, photographs, observations and breeding contributions remain part of the project's record.

Historical information should retain its chronology and distinguish recorded facts from later interpretations.

Photography and visual identification

Photographs and videos provide an important visual record of individual birds.

They may help document feather development, physical characteristics, posture, behaviour and changes that occur over time.

Original images and associated metadata may contain additional information beyond what can be preserved in a compressed video.

The project retains the distinction between original imagery, edited material and published visual representations.

Access to individual records

Continuing individual histories

The birds are not simply examples of a crossbreeding experiment. They are individual animals with their own lives, needs and histories.

Their documentation develops as new observations become available. Some histories will be extensive; others may remain incomplete.

The intention is to preserve individual identity and continuity without claiming more than the available records support.
